## On your points 3 and 4 — already how we report

Both of your presentation asks describe choices our reporting already makes, so there is nothing
for us to change, but it is worth saying how, in case the model is useful to you.

**Infrastructure errors never merge into product failures.** `docs/gates.json` names each broken
precondition with the error signature that identifies it in a run. Matching failures are reported
as *blocked*, never as lost coverage, and the two rates are published together: raw pass rate, and
pass rate of what was not blocked. Your note is the clearest possible argument for it — we built it
after one backend 500 turned into 39 red scenarios and read as a collapse from 95% to 44% with the
coverage behind those scenarios untouched. Today the DEF-10 gate takes our contradictions from 18
to 5 and reports "1 outage blocking 21 checks" instead.

**Claimed and proven are shown side by side and never merged.** Claimed is the curated status in
`surface-inventory.json`; proven is derived per run by joining surface → cited cases → catalog →
scenario name → result. Both sit in the same KPI strip with a verdict, so neither number can be
quoted as the headline on its own. A surface claimed covered but genuinely failing is a `conflict`
and fails the build; a surface claimed defective but now passing is `fix-detected` and also fails,
which is the automated version of the six stale rows you found by hand.

**No stale rows on our side today** — the equivalent check reads zero. Your note did prompt one
change: that check previously only displayed on the page rather than warning, which is exactly how
six of them could accumulate unnoticed.

## What your note cost us in fixes to our own tooling

Adding a `@bug` scenario broke our traceability gate in two ways, both real and both now fixed
(decision D24):

1. A failing `@bug` scenario counted as a **contradiction**, so any bugs-profile run would have
   failed CI with every open defect flagged as a false coverage claim.
2. After fixing that, the surface still read **"not proven"**, because one filtered case outranked
   two passing ones — so writing a defect down quietly demoted the surface it was found on.

Both were caught by the gate itself when AZ-19 landed.
